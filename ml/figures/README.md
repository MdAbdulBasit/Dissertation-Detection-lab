## ⭐ Figures 7 and 8 are the two that carry the argument

**Figure 7 replaces the Navigator screenshot for the chapter.** The exported layers render roughly 600
enterprise techniques in order to show 15; at A4 the technique labels are under 1 pt and the finding —
7 blind → 0, 3 discriminating → 1 — is invisible in the figure meant to carry it. Figure 7 shows the 15
measured techniques only, grouped by tactic, in two panels, with the four score levels as **discrete
labelled swatches** rather than a continuous 0–3 gradient (values like 0.43 and 1.7 do not exist in the
data). **The full-matrix SVGs in `navigator_layers/figures/` remain the appendix evidence and the JSON
remains the citable artefact** — they are the coverage claim; figure 7 is how a reader sees it.

**Figure 8 is the study's central claim, which previously had no visual at all.** Three pairs where the
same technique, sensor, author and day produce opposite results depending only on what the rule matches:

| Technique | Mechanism-keyed | Object-keyed |
|---|---|---|
| T1003.001 | `100320` **5 / 5** | `100321` **6 / 0** |
| T1112 | `100262` **10 / 10** | `100260` **20 / 0** |
| T1070.004 | `100310` **10 / 10** | `100311` **5 / 0** |

Counts are computed **in each technique's own windows**. ⚠️ `100310` additionally fires 9 benign alerts
inside T1003.001 windows, so its dataset-wide figure is **10 / 19** — the mechanism rule is worse than
the pair alone suggests, and only the within-technique count is a fair comparison.

T1003.001 is the strongest single data point in the study: the benign mirror runs a **character-identical
command line**, so nothing in the command line can separate the classes and only the sensor's
access-mask field does.

## Which figures carry the argument

**Figure 5 is the most striking result in the project.** Level 15 — the highest severity Wazuh can
assign — is **32% attack**, i.e. majority false positive. Level ≥ 4 flags **79.2% of benign** against
only **69.1% of attack**. The field every SOC triages on is *slightly anti-correlated* with truth.

**Figure 6** is the argument in one image: two of the three rule heuristics score **below** escalating
everything, and perfect ATT&CK attribution (0.412) does not help prioritisation at all.

**Figures 1 and 4 are the honesty pair.** Figure 1 shows the model misses 24.5% of attacks at the
default threshold; Figure 4 shows that fixing that costs most of the workload saving. Publish them
together — Figure 1 alone understates the problem and Figure 4 alone hides it.

## ⚠️ A shared cross-validated fit

Figures 1, 3 and 4 all derive from **one** call to `cross_val_predict`. A second call would refit five
Random Forests and could return marginally different probabilities — two figures in the same chapter
disagreeing about the same model is the kind of defect nobody checks for.

## ⚠️ Every figure was wrong on first render, and none of it showed in the logs

The script reported success six times while producing three unusable figures:

- **Figure 5** — the base-rate caption printed on top of the level-10 bar label.
- **Figure 6** — the caption ran into the x-axis and was unreadable.
- **Figure 3** — `severity ≥ 6` and `severity ≥ 8` differ by 0.008 recall, so their inline labels
  printed over each other. Two rounds of offset-tweaking still collided; fixed by moving all labels
  into the legend, which cannot overlap.

The data was correct in all three cases. **Layout defects are invisible to every check except looking
at the image** — the same lesson as the Navigator layers, which validated perfectly as JSON and rendered
five techniques out of fifteen. Re-render and *look* after any change to this script.
